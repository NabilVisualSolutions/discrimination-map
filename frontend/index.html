Symbol hunters

Banned symbols — swastikas, SS runes, "88"/"18" codes, the Reichskriegsflagge, banned-party logos — show up as graffiti and stickers in public space constantly, and mostly go undocumented. That's the gap this fills.

What counts

  • Graffiti, stickers, posters displaying a banned symbol (StGB §86a)
  • In public view — street furniture, walls, lamp posts, transit stops

Staying safe

  • Photograph from public space — you don't need to enter private property
  • Don't confront anyone putting these up; document and leave
  • Don't remove or deface the sticker/graffiti yourself — report it to the property owner or city instead, and leave evidence intact for that report
  • If you feel unsafe in the moment, leave first, document later or not at all

Filing it

  • Use "File a report" — category is already set to Banned symbol / salute
  • A photo link as evidence makes the report far more useful for review
  • Your report is public and anonymous, logged as unverified pending review

What this is

Hate incidents don't stop being real when the news cycle moves on. This is where the pattern stays visible.

Documentation, not accusation

Every mark is a sourced, unverified lead — never a verdict about a named person. Presumption of innocence, always.

History isn't deleted

Older reports fade visually after they age but are never removed. A quiet pattern is still a pattern.

Built for the people in it

Fuzzed locations for sensitive reports, a quick-exit button, and real crisis resources — on every screen, not buried in a footer.

Three moving parts

  • People report directly — anyone can file a report anonymously, as much or as little detail as they're comfortable sharing.
  • Public sources are scanned — free, public social platforms are monitored for relevant posts, stored as unverified leads pending review.
  • The pattern becomes visible — filter by category and watch how a region's pattern actually developed, not just its most recent headline.
