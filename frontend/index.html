Report an incident

Document a hate incident. Give what you can — the more evidence, the more useful. Reports are public and anonymous. Report crimes to your local emergency services.

Don't include the name of any private person — victim or accused. Describe what happened, not who specifically did it.

Tip: right-click a spot on the map (or press and hold on mobile) to pin a report right there.

Symbol hunters

Banned symbols — swastikas, SS runes, "88"/"18" codes, the Reichskriegsflagge, banned-party logos — show up as graffiti and stickers in public space constantly, and mostly go undocumented. That's the gap this fills.

What counts

  • Graffiti, stickers, posters displaying a banned symbol (§ 86a StGB)
  • In public view — street furniture, walls, lamp posts, transit stops

Staying safe

  • Photograph from public space — you don't need to enter private property
  • Don't confront anyone putting these up; document and leave
  • Don't remove or deface the sticker/graffiti yourself — report it to the property owner or city instead
  • If you feel unsafe in the moment, leave first, document later or not at all

Filing it

  • Use "Report" — category is already set to Banned symbol / salute
  • A photo link as evidence makes the report far more useful for review
  • Your report is public and anonymous, logged as unverified pending review

What this is

Hate incidents don't stop being real when the news cycle moves on. This is where the pattern stays visible.

Documentation, not accusation

Every mark is a sourced, unverified lead — never a verdict about a named person. Presumption of innocence, always.

History isn't deleted

Older reports fade visually after they age but are never removed. A quiet pattern is still a pattern.

Built for the people in it

Fuzzed locations for unverified reports and real crisis resources — on every screen, not buried in a footer.

Always anonymous

No names, faces, or addresses of private individuals appear on the map — neither the person reporting nor the person a report is about. This documents patterns and events, never people.

Nothing publishes itself

A report only reaches the public map once it either carries real, checkable evidence (a source link) or an admin/reviewer has looked at it — an anonymous claim alone is never enough on its own.

Three moving parts

  • People report directly — anyone can file a report anonymously.
  • Public sources are scanned — public platforms are monitored for relevant posts.
  • The pattern becomes visible — filter by category and watch the pattern develop.
