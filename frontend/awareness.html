Awareness

Recognizing far-right symbols, gestures, and coded language.

Not a catalog to browse for its own sake — a reference for recognizing what you're looking at, so you can report it, avoid it, or just understand what you saw.

Why this page describes rather than shows

You won't find images of the symbols here. Text descriptions are enough for recognition, and reproducing the imagery itself — even for education — risks doing the one thing this project exists to stop. Everything below is grounded in Germany's actual statute references (the same ones the map uses to classify reports), not a general internet list.

Banned under § 86a StGB

Symbols and gestures

Public use or display of these is a criminal offense in Germany, outside narrow exceptions for art, science, research, or reporting on history.

§ 86a StGB

The swastika (Hakenkreuz)

The most widely recognized Nazi symbol. Any orientation, any context outside the legal exceptions above, is covered by the ban — this includes stylized or partially-obscured versions used to evade moderation.

§ 86a StGB

SS runes and the death's head (Totenkopf)

The double lightning-bolt "Sig rune" associated with the SS, and the skull-and-crossbones insignia used by SS units, are both banned symbols under the same statute.

§ 86a StGB

The Hitler salute and "Sieg Heil"

The raised-arm salute, and the phrase "Sieg Heil" spoken or written publicly, are both criminalized gestures/speech — not just symbols in the graphic sense.

§ 86a StGB

The Reichskriegsflagge (war ensign)

Specific historical war-flag variants (particularly the 1938 pattern with the Iron Cross and swastika) are banned; the flag has been adopted as a stand-in symbol by later far-right groups specifically because the plain naval-ensign version is not automatically illegal — context and specific variant matter.

Coded language

Numbers and letter codes

Numeric substitution ciphers let the same message travel as a tattoo, a license plate, or a screen name without tripping an obvious keyword filter. None of these numbers are illegal on their own — context is what matters — but recognizing the pattern is the point.

88
H is the 8th letter — "HH" = "Heil Hitler"
18
"AH" = Adolf Hitler's initials
14 / 1488
The "14 words" white-supremacist slogan, often paired with 88
28
"BH" — Blood & Honour, a banned network
Organizations

Banned associations

Germany's federal and state governments can and do ban specific far-right organizations under the Vereinsgesetz (Associations Act) — continuing to act for or promote a banned association is itself a separate offense (§ 20 VereinsG). Which organizations are currently banned changes over time as courts and interior ministries act — the map's own statute reference stays current with what's classifiable, but for the authoritative, up-to-date list, the Bundesamt für Verfassungsschutz (Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution) publishes one directly.

If you see one of these

Graffiti, a sticker, a symbol in public — document it from a safe distance, on public property, without confronting anyone. If it's a crime in progress or you feel unsafe, call the police (110) first. Otherwise, the map has a reporting flow built for exactly this.
