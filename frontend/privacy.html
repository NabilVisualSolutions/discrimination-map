What we collect

  • Scraped public posts. Text and links from public Bluesky/Mastodon posts our monitoring agent finds — nothing private, nothing behind a login. We keep the post text, a link back to the original, and (where we can determine one) an approximate location. The link to the original contains the author's public account name. We also read public pages from Wikipedia and the GDELT news index (title, link, publisher and date), and, only where the operator has switched them on, Reddit and YouTube search results. News feeds (RSS) are being added: for each item we store the headline, the link, the publisher, the date and a snippet of at most 160 characters.
  • Reports you submit. Whatever you type into the report form, and the location you place on the map. No name, no email, no account required — reports are anonymous by design.
  • Your edit link. If you submit a report, a one-time edit token is generated and saved only in your own browser's local storage — it never leaves your device again except when you use it to edit that specific report. We can't recover it for you; if you clear your browser data, that report is no longer editable by you. The token is kept for one hour, the same window in which the server accepts edits from it; after that the report can no longer be edited by you.
  • Volunteer and reviewer accounts. If you apply to volunteer or are invited as a moderator, we store your name, email, your message, the time you accepted these terms and a securely hashed password (never the password itself), plus a session cookie while you're logged in. To check the address is yours we email you a one-time link through our mail provider (Google Workspace, SMTP); the link is stored only as a hash and expires after 48 hours, and we record when it was opened. An application you never confirm can be deleted on request. If you sign in with Google, Google (Google LLC, USA) handles that sign-in and sends us your name and email address, which we use to find or create your account.
  • Basic server logs. Standard technical logs (request timestamps, IP addresses) kept briefly for security and abuse prevention — not linked to reports you submit. Optional usage statistics. Only if you press Accept in the banner, we count visits and which tabs are used with Amplitude (servers in the EU, legal basis: your consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and § 25 TDDDG). No click or typing recording, no session replay, IP address not stored, and never what you report. The choice is saved only in your browser; the "Privacy choices" link in Get involved lets you withdraw it at any time. Our traffic passes through Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., USA), which relays requests and therefore sees visitor IP addresses. To limit abuse, the app also keeps short-lived per-address request counters in memory only; they are not written to the database. Where the bot check on the report and sign-up forms (Cloudflare Turnstile) is switched on, it is loaded from challenges.cloudflare.com and receives your IP address and browser data; the legal basis is our legitimate interest in protecting the forms from abuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Map tiles

The map background comes from OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org). Your browser requests it directly, so OpenFreeMap receives your IP address and the usual request data (browser type, the tiles requested), as any web server does. If you choose the satellite view, the images are NASA GIBS imagery, but your browser does not contact NASA: it asks our own server, which fetches the images from NASA and keeps a copy for between one hour and seven days. NASA only ever sees our server, never your IP address or browser data. Our server sees your IP address like any web server and uses it only to limit abuse. The map sets no cookies for this. It keeps your language and display choices (view, 3D, panel) only in your browser's local storage; they are not sent to us. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in showing a map (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Fonts

The map's typefaces (Sora and Fragment Mono) come from Bunny Fonts (fonts.bunny.net), an EU-based font service that replaces Google Fonts. Your browser requests them directly, so Bunny receives your IP address and the usual request data (browser type, the font files requested), as any web server does. We do not use Google Fonts. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in displaying the site as designed (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Your location, place search and source previews

Your location. The map asks your browser for your position only after you press the My location button (and, in the report form, the Use my location button). Your browser then asks for your permission. The position is used on your device to move the map and is not sent to us, unless you submit a report with that place.

Place search. When you search for a place, the text you type is sent to our server, which asks Nominatim (OpenStreetMap, run by the OpenStreetMap Foundation, United Kingdom) and returns the result. Nominatim receives the search text and our server's address, not your IP address. Our server keeps recent results in memory for a short time.

Source previews. An incident can offer a preview of its source (YouTube through youtube-nocookie.com, Bluesky, Mastodon or Wikipedia). Nothing is loaded from that provider until you click the preview. After your click your browser contacts the provider directly, which receives your IP address and browser data and may store or read data on your device under its own privacy policy. Several of these providers are US companies. The legal basis is your click (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, § 25 TDDDG). You can use the link to the original source instead.

Why we collect it, and on what legal basis

Documenting publicly-posted incidents and events is done under legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) — the public-interest value of making a pattern visible, weighed against minimal privacy impact since the source posts were already public. Reviewer accounts exist under the same basis, to run the moderation process at all.

A note on sensitive content. Some entries reference alleged criminal conduct (e.g. symbols banned under German law). We treat these strictly as unverified leads, never as an assertion of guilt, and hold anonymous, unverifiable submissions out of the public map until a human reviews them.

How long we keep it

Reports aren't deleted on a timer — the project's value is in showing patterns over years, and older entries visually fade rather than disappear so the map stays honest about what's recent versus historical. You can ask for a specific report to be removed at any time (see below); dismissed reports (spam, false positives) are kept internally for moderation records but never shown publicly. Other time limits: sign-in sessions expire automatically, email confirmation links expire after 48 hours, the edit token in your browser lasts one hour, and the content-free record that a report was deleted (who, when, which fields were edited by whom) is removed after 365 days.

Your rights

Under GDPR and comparable laws, you can ask to access, correct, or delete data about you, object to or restrict our processing, and lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority (in Germany: the state DPA where you live, or the BfDI). Where we rely on your consent you can withdraw it at any time. The authority responsible for us is the Berlin Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information.

If you submitted a report yourself, self-editing is built in (see above) for one hour after you submit it. For anything else — including a request to remove a report you're mentioned in — email dxmap@nabilvs.com and we'll respond directly.

Sharing and security

We don't sell data or share it with advertisers. Our hosting infrastructure processes data as needed to run the service. Passwords are hashed (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, never stored in plain text), sessions expire automatically, and location data for the most sensitive report categories is deliberately blurred before it's ever shown publicly. Service providers that handle or relay data for us include Cloudflare, our server host, Google (sign-in and email) and, only with your consent, Amplitude; some of them are US companies.

Not for children

This project isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 16.
