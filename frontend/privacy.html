Privacy Policy

What we collect, why, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it.

Last updated: August 2026
This page is written to be clear and complete, not to be the last word — if you have a specific legal question, talk to a lawyer in your jurisdiction. If anything here is unclear, email us and we'll explain or fix it.

Who runs this

Discrimination Map is an independent documentation project. Questions, corrections, or requests about your data go to dxmap@nabilvs.com.

What we collect

  • Scraped public posts. Text and links from public Bluesky/Mastodon posts our monitoring agent finds — nothing private, nothing behind a login. We keep the post text, a link back to the original, and (where we can determine one) an approximate location.
  • Reports you submit. Whatever you type into the report form, and the location you place on the map. No name, no email, no account required — reports are anonymous by design.
  • Your edit link. If you submit a report, a one-time edit token is generated and saved only in your own browser's local storage — it never leaves your device again except when you use it to edit that specific report. We can't recover it for you; if you clear your browser data, that report is no longer editable by you.
  • Reviewer accounts. If you're invited as a moderator, we store your email and a securely hashed password (never the password itself) plus a session cookie while you're logged in.
  • Basic server logs. Standard technical logs (request timestamps, IP addresses) kept briefly for security and abuse prevention — not linked to reports you submit.

Why we collect it, and on what legal basis

Documenting publicly-posted incidents and events is done under legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) — the public-interest value of making a pattern visible, weighed against minimal privacy impact since the source posts were already public. Reviewer accounts exist under the same basis, to run the moderation process at all.

A note on sensitive content. Some entries reference alleged criminal conduct (e.g. symbols banned under German law). We treat these strictly as unverified leads, never as an assertion of guilt, and hold anonymous, unverifiable submissions out of the public map until a human reviews them.

How long we keep it

Reports aren't deleted on a timer — the project's value is in showing patterns over years, and older entries visually fade rather than disappear so the map stays honest about what's recent versus historical. You can ask for a specific report to be removed at any time (see below); dismissed reports (spam, false positives) are kept internally for moderation records but never shown publicly.

Your rights

Under GDPR and comparable laws, you can ask to access, correct, or delete data about you, object to or restrict our processing, and lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority (in Germany: the state DPA where you live, or the BfDI).

If you submitted a report yourself, self-editing is built in (see above) while it's still pending review. For anything else — including a request to remove a report you're mentioned in — email dxmap@nabilvs.com and we'll respond directly.

Sharing and security

We don't sell data or share it with advertisers. Our hosting infrastructure processes data as needed to run the service. Passwords are hashed (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, never stored in plain text), sessions expire automatically, and location data for the most sensitive report categories is deliberately blurred before it's ever shown publicly.

Not for children

This project isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 16.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a meaningful way, we'll update the date at the top of this page.

Questions about your data? Email dxmap@nabilvs.com. See also our Terms of Use.
