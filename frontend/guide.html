Volunteer guide

Applying, getting verified, and getting started.

Five steps from "I'd like to help" to reviewing reports or contributing code.

1

Apply

Go to nabilvs.com's Discrimination Map project page, scroll to "Get involved."

Already signed into nabilvs.com? Your name and email fill in automatically — no retyping, no confirmation email needed.

Not signed in? Fill in your name, email, how you'd like to help (Moderator, Translator, Coder, Organization partner, Symbol hunter, or Other), an optional message, pass the captcha, and submit.

2

Confirm your email

Only if you applied signed out. Check your inbox for an email from dxmap@nabilvs.com and click the confirm link — it just proves the email is really yours.

3

Create a nabilvs.com account (or log in)

Use the same email you applied with — email/password, Google, or GitHub all work. This is your nabilvs.com account, not a separate one for the map.

4

Check your status

Dashboard → Volunteering. Shows whether your email is confirmed, and your application status: Pending review → In progress → Approved (or Not moving forward).

5

If approved as a moderator

You'll get a separate login for map.nabilvs.com/admin — the actual tool for reviewing reports. Different login from your nabilvs.com account, on purpose: a different system, kept simple and separate.

Just want to document a banned symbol right now?

No application needed. Open the map, tap Report, category "Banned symbol / salute." Anonymous, no account.

Editing

Already filed a report and need to fix something?

Tap the marker you filed — it stays visible to you (and only you) right after you submit it, even while it's pending review. If an "Edit my report" button shows up, you can still change it.

This only works from the same browser and device you used to file it, and only until a reviewer has looked at it — reports are anonymous by design, so there's no account we can use to recover the edit link for you later.

New category

Solidarity events aren't harm reports

Counter-protests, vigils, and solidarity rallies against far-right extremism get their own category — shown as a gold star on the map, not a colored dot. If you're reviewing, treat these differently from the other nine: they document a positive public event, not an alleged incident, so there's no location fuzzing and nothing to "verify" in the same sense.

This is also the one category where every report on the map connects into a single nationwide network, not just nearby ones — every solidarity event, filed anywhere, joins one continuous web showing the whole resistance at a glance.
