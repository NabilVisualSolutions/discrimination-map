A public, documented, honest map

Hate incidents don't stop being real when the news cycle moves on.
This is where the pattern stays visible.

Discrimination Map documents racism, antisemitism, Islamophobia, homophobia, Neo-Nazi activity, harassment, and sexual violence over time and place — so the shape of the problem is visible, not just the latest headline.

How it works

Three moving parts

No single source is treated as ground truth — the map is only as honest as its weakest input, so every input is labeled for what it actually is.

01

People report directly

Anyone can file a report anonymously — as much or as little detail as they're comfortable sharing.

02

Public sources are scanned

Free, public social platforms are monitored for relevant public posts, which are stored as unverified leads pending review.

03

The pattern becomes visible

Filter by category, scrub the timeline, and watch how a region's pattern actually developed — not just its most recent headline.

Safety & ethics

What we won't compromise on

This is a documentation tool, not an emergency service

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. The map exists to make long-term patterns visible — it is not built or staffed for crisis response.

Reports are leads, never verdicts

Nothing on the map asserts guilt or confirms an allegation. Automated and user-submitted reports are explicitly labeled unverified, pending human review for the most sensitive categories.

The map documents incidents, not people

No names, faces, or addresses of private individuals — on either side of a report. Locations for sexual violence and harassment reports are deliberately approximated, never exact.

Discrimination Map is a documentation and awareness project. It is not a substitute for law enforcement, legal advice, or crisis counseling. Automated and user-submitted content reflects unverified reports and should be treated as such.
